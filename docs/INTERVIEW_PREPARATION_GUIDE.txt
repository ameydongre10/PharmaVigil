2. PHARMACOVIGILANCE & REGULATORY DOMAIN FUNDAMENTALS
================================================================================

Q: What is Pharmacovigilance (PV)?
A: The science and activities relating to the detection, assessment, understanding,
   and prevention of adverse effects or any other drug-related problem.

Q: What is an ICSR?
A: Individual Case Safety Report. A standardized record describing one adverse event
   experience reported by a patient, doctor, or pharmacist concerning one individual.

Q: What is a PADER?
A: Periodic Adverse Drug Experience Report. Under FDA 21 CFR § 314.80(c)(2), holders
   of approved New Drug Applications (NDAs) or ANDAs must submit quarterly reports
   for the first 3 years post-approval, and annual reports thereafter.

Q: What are the Mandatory Sections of a PADER?
A: 1. Narrative Summary and Analysis of Information in the Report
   2. Summary Analysis of Cases (Volume, Demographics, Serious vs Non-serious)
   3. Reaction Analysis (Most frequent MedDRA Preferred Terms)
   4. 15-Day Alert Reports (Expedited cases submitted within 15 days)
   5. Trends and Important Observations
   6. History of Actions Taken (e.g. label changes, clinical holds)
   7. Case Listing (Tabular listing of individual safety report IDs)
   8. Reporting Period (Exact date window of cases received)

Q: What is a 15-Day Alert Report?
A: Adverse drug experiences that are BOTH serious and unexpected must be reported to
   the FDA within 15 calendar days of initial receipt. In the PADER, all expedited
   alerts submitted during the interval must be summarized.

Q: What is Expectedness?
A: Whether an adverse event is already listed in the official approved product label
   (Company Core Data Sheet / CCDS). If it is not listed, it is 'unexpected'. If no
   product label is provided in the input data, you CANNOT determine expectedness.
   PharmaVigil explicitly declares this as an out-of-scope limitation statement.

Q: What is MedDRA?
A: Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities.
   Hierarchical terminology used in drug safety:
   - SOC (System Organ Class) e.g., Cardiac Disorders
   - HLGT (High Level Group Term)
   - HLT (High Level Term)
   - PT (Preferred Term) e.g., "Bradycardia", "Acute kidney injury"
   Note: The input ICSR dataset in this challenge only contains PT level data
   (`patient_reaction_reactionmeddrapt`), not SOC. PharmaVigil correctly refrains
   from hallucinating SOC categories!

Q: The Golden Rule: ROW != CASE != REACTION
A: - Row: A single line in the CSV or Excel file.
   - Case: A unique patient safety report identified by `safetyreportid`.
   - Reaction: A specific adverse event reported for that patient.
   One patient (Case) can experience 3 different reactions, resulting in 3 rows
   in the file. If you count rows as cases, you severely corrupt all statistics!
   PharmaVigil deduplicates by `safetyreportid` for patient metrics and by
   `(safetyreportid, reactionmeddrapt)` for reaction frequency.

3. WHY NAIVE GENAI / CHATGPT FAILS (AND HOW PHARMAVIGIL WINS)
================================================================================

Failure Mode 1: Arithmetic Hallucinations
- Problem: LLMs are probabilistic token predictors, not calculators. If asked
  "What is the percentage of serious cases in 1024 cases where 1023 are serious?",
  they often output 99.8%, 99.91%, or round unpredictably.
- PharmaVigil Solution: Pure Python arithmetic calculates `round((1023/1024)*100, 2) = 99.9%`.
  The calculated value is provided to the LLM as immutable evidence `EV_CASE_SERIOUS_PCT`.

Failure Mode 2: Premature Causality Inference
- Problem: Generic prompts cause LLMs to declare "Bisoprolol caused renal failure."
  In pharmacovigilance, an adverse event is an observation, not proof of causality.
- PharmaVigil Solution: Grounding instructions explicitly state:
  "Do NOT infer causality or declare safety signals unless the evidence explicitly
   supports such a conclusion. Use neutral regulatory language."

Failure Mode 3: Prompt Context Stuffing & Noise
- Problem: Dumping a 1,068-row table into a prompt wastes tokens, causes context
  drift, and results in models missing rare but critical adverse reactions.
- PharmaVigil Solution: Context packet architecture. Each section receives ONLY
  the specific pre-aggregated evidence items it requires.

Failure Mode 4: Self-Approval / Compliance Bypass
- Problem: An AI system that claims "Report approved for FDA submission" is illegal.
- PharmaVigil Solution: Reports start strictly as `DRAFT_PENDING_REVIEW`. Automated
  validation passing produces `automated_evaluation_status = PASS`, but
  `is_human_approved` remains `False` until an authenticated medical officer signs.

4. PHARMAVIGIL ARCHITECTURE (THE 6 PHASES)
================================================================================

PHASE 1: INGESTION & NORMALIZATION (`src/data_loader.py`)
- Accepts `.xlsx` or `.csv`.
- Lowercases and strips column whitespace.
- Normalizes boolean flags: `serious` ("1", "yes", "true" -> True; else False).
- Normalizes expedited flags: `fulfillexpeditecriteria` ("1", "yes" -> True).
- Standardizes country codes (fallback from `occurcountry` to `primarysource_reportercountry`).
- Normalizes age to float, rejecting impossible ages (<0 or >150).

PHASE 2: DETERMINISTIC ANALYSIS LAYER (`src/analysis_engine.py`)
- Builds `self.cases_df` by grouping on `safetyreportid`.
- Executes 13 deterministic analyses:
  * `get_reporting_period()`: Min and max receivedate.
  * `get_case_volume()`: Total cases, serious count, non-serious count, percentages.
  * `get_age_distribution()`: Bucketed into <18, 18-64, 65+, Unknown.
  * `get_sex_distribution()`: Case-level breakdown.
  * `get_country_distribution()`: Case-level geographic breakdown.
  * `get_reactions(top_n=20)`: Deduplicated count of unique PTs per case.
  * `get_serious_reactions(top_n=20)`: PT counts within serious cases.
  * `get_outcomes()`: Row-level outcome counts (recovered, fatal, etc.).
  * `get_time_trends()`: Monthly case volume counts.
  * `get_alert_cases()`: Expedited 15-day alert totals.
  * `get_history_of_actions()`: Out of scope declaration.
  * `get_expectedness()`: Out of scope declaration.
  * `get_case_index(limit=100)`: Case listing summary.
- Wraps every output in a structured `AnalysisResult` schema.

PHASE 3: EVIDENCE & CONTEXT ENGINEERING (`src/evidence_collector.py`, `src/context_builder.py`)
- Converts analysis results into atomic `EvidenceItem` objects with unique IDs:
  `EV_PERIOD_START`, `EV_CASE_TOTAL`, `EV_CASE_SERIOUS_PCT`, `EV_TOP_REACTIONS`, etc.
- ContextBuilder maps `PADER_CONFIG` section definitions to required evidence IDs.
- Generates section-specific `SectionContextPacket` objects.
- Injects non-negotiable regulatory grounding instructions into each packet.

PHASE 4: GROUNDED GENERATION & AUDIT (`src/section_generator.py`, `src/grounding_validator.py`)
- LLM Provider Abstraction (`src/llm_client.py`):
  * `MockLLMClient`: Offline deterministic generator for unit testing & demo.
  * `OpenAICompatibleLLMClient`: Zero-third-party-dependency client using standard
    `urllib.request` that connects to OpenAI, Gemini, Anthropic, or Ollama endpoints.
- Prompts instruct the LLM to output structured JSON matching `GeneratedSection`:
  `{ "narrative": "...", "evidence_ids": [...], "limitations": [...] }`
- GroundingValidator runs immediately post-generation:
  * Regex extracts all numeric figures from the narrative: `\b\d+(?:\.\d+)?\b`.
  * Filters out dates (e.g. 2024, 2025).
  * Validates every single extracted number against numbers in the evidence packet.
  * If an unverified number appears, sets `is_grounded = False` and logs warning.

PHASE 5: REGULATORY REPORT ASSEMBLY (`src/report_assembler.py`, `src/evaluator.py`)
- Combines deterministic tables, LLM narrative summaries, limitations, and evidence IDs
  into a compliant regulatory Markdown document.
- Report includes: Cover Page, Table of Contents, 8 Required Sections, 1 Limitation
  Notice (Expectedness), and Appendix.
- PADEREvaluator checks:
  * Completeness: 8/8 required sections present.
  * Factual consistency score (0-100%).
  * Grounding score (0-100%).
  * Automated status: `PASS` or `REVIEW_REQUIRED`.

PHASE 6: AUDIT TRAIL & HUMAN REVIEW (`src/human_review.py`)
- Sets initial report status to `DRAFT_PENDING_REVIEW`.
- `apply_decision()` records explicit human action:
  * `APPROVED` -> Sets status to `HUMAN_APPROVED`, `is_human_approved = True`.
  * `NEEDS_REVISION` -> Sets status to `NEEDS_REVISION`.
  * `REJECTED` -> Sets status to `REJECTED`.
  * `SIMULATED_APPROVED` -> Explicitly flags `is_simulated = True` (test fixture only).
- `regenerate_section()`: Allows surgical regeneration of a single section without
  rerunning the entire pipeline. Automatically resets status back to `DRAFT_PENDING_REVIEW`!

5. THE 8 REGULATORY RULES (RULES A TO H)
================================================================================

In an interview, referencing these rules shows deep system design discipline:

- Rule A: A newly generated report ALWAYS starts as `DRAFT_PENDING_REVIEW`.
- Rule B: Automated evaluator `PASS` does NOT change review status to `HUMAN_APPROVED`.
- Rule C: Only an explicit human review sign-off can set `HUMAN_APPROVED`.
- Rule D: A reviewer can set `NEEDS_REVISION` with required revision notes.
- Rule E: A reviewer can set `REJECTED` with rejection justification.
- Rule F: Simulated test fixtures must be explicitly distinguishable from real
          human approval (`is_simulated = True`).
- Rule G: Completeness checks must strictly enforce all 8 required assignment sections.
- Rule H: Expectedness (missing CCDS label) must be tracked as an additional limitation
          notice, NOT marked as a failed required section.

7. STEP-BY-STEP END-TO-END DATA LIFECYCLE (WALKTHROUGH)
================================================================================

When an interviewer asks: "Walk me through what happens under the hood when a user
uploads an Excel file and generates a report", describe this exact 10-step flow:

Step 1: Ingestion & Multipart Streaming
  - User drops an ICSR Excel file (`.xlsx`) onto the GitHub Pages UI.
  - The frontend streams it as `multipart/form-data` to `POST /api/upload` on the
    Render FastAPI backend.
  - FastAPI writes it to a safe temporary location, runs `load_and_normalize_data`,
    validates required columns (`safetyreportid`, `receivedate`, `patient_reaction_reactionmeddrapt`),
    and returns metadata (case volume, date range, row count).

Step 2: Triggering the Pipeline Orchestrator
  - User selects LLM execution mode (Mock or live API key) and clicks "Run Pipeline".
  - The frontend sends a JSON payload to `POST /api/generate`.
  - `PADEROrchestrator` initializes `SectionGenerator`, `GroundingValidator`,
    `ReportAssembler`, `PADEREvaluator`, and `HumanReviewManager`.

Step 3: Deterministic Analysis Aggregation
  - `AnalysisEngine` ingests the normalized DataFrame.
  - It creates `self.cases_df` (deduplicating by `safetyreportid`) and computes 13
    exact metrics (Reporting Period min/max, Case Volume, Age Buckets, Sex Breakdown,
    Country Counts, MedDRA Reaction Frequencies, 15-Day Expedited Alerts).

Step 4: Evidence Synthesis
  - `collect_evidence()` packages the mathematical results into typed `EvidenceItem`
    objects with immutable string IDs (`EV_CASE_TOTAL`, `EV_TOP_REACTIONS`, etc.).

Step 5: Context Packet Assembly
  - `ContextBuilder` inspects `PADER_CONFIG` to determine which evidence IDs are
    authorized for each section.
  - It constructs a `SectionContextPacket` for Narrative Summary, Reaction Analysis,
    15-Day Alert Reports, and Trends, injecting non-negotiable prompt guardrails.

Step 6: LLM Narrative Synthesis
  - `SectionGenerator` invokes the LLM client with system prompts and context JSON.
  - The LLM returns structured JSON matching `GeneratedSection` (narrative text,
    cited evidence IDs, declared limitations).

Step 7: Real-Time Regex Grounding Audit
  - `GroundingValidator` receives the generated section.
  - It runs regex extraction on every number in the narrative, filters out dates,
    and cross-checks the remaining numbers against the approved evidence pool.
  - If any number is unsupported, it flags the claim and logs warnings.

Step 8: Document Assembly & Completeness Evaluation
  - `ReportAssembler` merges deterministic markdown tables (Case volume, Reaction
    ranking tables, Case index) with grounded narrative paragraphs.
  - `PADEREvaluator` validates that all 8 mandatory PADER sections are present and
    calculates factual consistency and grounding scores.
  - The report status is locked to `DRAFT_PENDING_REVIEW`.

Step 9: Database Caching & Frontend Streaming
  - The assembled `PADERReport` object is cached in `REPORTS_DB`.
  - The API responds with report metadata, evaluation metrics, and markdown text.
  - Marked.js renders the regulatory document with styled tables and charts on
    the GitHub Pages UI.

Step 10: Human Medical Sign-Off
  - The medical officer reviews the document and clicks "Record Reviewer Decision".
  - `POST /api/reports/{id}/review` is invoked with decision `APPROVED`, reviewer
    credentials, and audit notes.
  - `HumanReviewManager` transitions the report to `HUMAN_APPROVED` and generates
    an immutable `review.json` audit trail.

10. TOP 25 TOUGH INTERVIEW QUESTIONS & MODEL ANSWERS
================================================================================

Q1: What was the motivation behind PharmaVigil?
A1: "In pharmacovigilance, medical writers spend weeks manually cross-referencing
     ICSR line-listings to author periodic safety reports. Standard generative AI
     cannot be trusted here because it hallucinates statistics and invents causality.
     I built PharmaVigil to decouple statistical computation from narrative synthesis:
     deterministic code computes all numbers, and the LLM is restricted to drafting
     grounded regulatory prose from pre-verified evidence."

Q2: Why not just use Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)?
A2: "Standard RAG retrieves text chunks based on semantic similarity. But tabular
     safety data isn't a text retrieval problem—it's an aggregation and counting
     problem. Semantic search cannot tell you if there are 22 or 23 cases of Acute
     kidney injury. You need deterministic grouping and deduplication across cases
     and reactions, which standard RAG vector databases cannot do."

Q3: How does your Grounding Validator detect hallucinations?
A3: "When the LLM outputs a section narrative, GroundingValidator runs a regex pattern
     `\b\d+(?:\.\d+)?\b` to extract every single integer and floating-point number.
     It filters out legitimate dates (like 2024, 2025) and cross-checks the remaining
     values against a normalized set of numbers present in the approved EvidenceItem
     objects for that section. If an unsupported number appears, the section is flagged,
     and the factual consistency score is penalized."

Q4: Explain the distinction between Rows, Cases, and Reactions in your dataset.
A4: "In FAERS and clinical safety data, each row represents a reaction occurrence.
     A single patient—identified uniquely by `safetyreportid`—may experience multiple
     reactions like Bradycardia and Hypotension. If you count rows as cases, you double-
     or triple-count patients. In PharmaVigil, `AnalysisEngine` builds a deduplicated
     `cases_df` for volume, demographics, and outcomes, while using row-level data
     deduplicated by `(safetyreportid, reactionmeddrapt)` for reaction frequency."

Q5: Why is the Expectedness section treated differently in your pipeline?
A5: "Expectedness measures whether an adverse reaction is already listed in the approved
     drug label (CCDS). In the challenge dataset, no product label or CCDS was provided.
     Rather than letting an LLM guess or hallucinate what's on a label, PharmaVigil
     explicitly quarantines Expectedness as an official limitation notice, separate
     from the 8 mandatory PADER sections. This complies with FDA submission transparency."

Q6: How do you prevent an AI model from self-approving a regulatory report?
A6: "Through state-machine enforcement in `PADEROrchestrator` and `HumanReviewManager`.
     A newly generated report is assigned `status = ReportStatus.DRAFT_PENDING_REVIEW`
     and `is_human_approved = False`. Even if all automated checks PASS with 100% scores,
     the report cannot transition to `HUMAN_APPROVED` without an explicit call to the
     review endpoint with reviewer credentials and sign-off rationale."

Q7: What happens if a reviewer requests changes?
A7: "The reviewer submits a `NEEDS_REVISION` decision with audit notes. PharmaVigil
     supports surgical single-section regeneration via `regenerate_section()`. The
     system regenerates only that specific section and automatically resets the report
     status back to `DRAFT_PENDING_REVIEW` to ensure the final document is re-reviewed."

Q8: How did you implement the LLM provider abstraction?
A8: "I designed an abstract base class `LLMClient` with a `generate(LLMRequest)` method.
     I implemented two concrete classes: `MockLLMClient`, which deterministically extracts
     evidence from prompts for fast, zero-cost, offline testing, and `OpenAICompatibleLLMClient`,
     which uses Python's standard `urllib.request` library without any third-party SDK
     dependencies to connect to OpenAI, Google Gemini, Anthropic, or local Ollama servers."

Q9: How is the application deployed in production?
A9: "It uses a decoupled cloud architecture:
     The backend is containerized via Docker and deployed on Render as a FastAPI web
     service with automatic health checks (`/api/health`).
     The frontend is a lightweight Single-Page Application deployed on GitHub Pages
     via a GitHub Actions CI/CD workflow. The frontend communicates with the Render API
     over HTTPS with full CORS configuration."

Q10: What did you do to handle Render's free tier cold starts?
A10: "Render free instances spin down after 15 minutes of inactivity and take ~30-45s
      to boot back up. On GitHub Pages, I added an automated health-probe listener with
      an exponential retry loop. When a user opens the site while the backend is asleep,
      an informative banner appears explaining the wakeup process with an active countdown,
      preventing confusion or broken UI states."

Q11: How many tests do you have and what do they cover?
A11: "120 automated tests running under `pytest` with 100% passing rate in ~5 seconds.
      They cover:
      - Unit tests for deterministic analysis and demographic bucketing (`test_analysis.py`).
      - Context packet building and evidence filtering (`test_evidence.py`).
      - Grounded LLM section generation and validation schemas (`test_generation.py`).
      - End-to-end orchestration and human review transitions (`test_pipeline.py`).
      - REST API endpoints and file upload validation (`test_api.py`).
      - 1,036 lines of hardening tests covering malformed inputs, missing columns,
        simulated failures, and regression assertions on authoritative counts (`test_hardening.py`)."

Q12: What was the most challenging bug you encountered and resolved?
A12: "When the repository was originally uploaded to GitHub via web upload, all folder
      hierarchies were flattened into the root directory. Because internal modules used
      relative imports (`from .models import AnalysisResult`), every script and test broke
      with `ImportError: attempted relative import with no known parent package`.
      I diagnosed the package structure, restored the `src/` package and `tests/` directories
      using `git mv` to preserve git history, added `pytest.ini` with `pythonpath = .`,
      and resolved all import resolutions across the test suite."

Q13: Why did you choose FastAPI over Flask or Django?
A13: "FastAPI provides native asynchronous ASGI performance, automatic Pydantic v2
      request/response schema validation, and automatically generates interactive Swagger
      and ReDoc documentation (`/docs`). For a regulatory tool where typed schemas and
      machine-readable contracts are paramount, FastAPI is the industry standard."

Q14: How does the system handle security and credentials?
A14: "Zero hardcoded secrets. The application reads configuration from environment
      variables via `.env`. In the web dashboard, API keys can either be configured
      server-side as environment variables or provided per-request in the browser session
      without ever being written to persistent disk."

Q15: Can PharmaVigil handle other drugs besides Bisoprolol, or larger datasets?
A15: "Yes, completely drug-agnostic. The ingestion layer dynamically adapts to any
      dataset matching the FDA E2B/FAERS ICSR schema (`safetyreportid`, `receivedate`,
      `patient_reaction_reactionmeddrapt`, `serious`, `fulfillexpeditecriteria`).
      For massive multi-million row FAERS quarterly archives, the deterministic engine
      can be adapted to Polars or DuckDB for sub-second aggregations."

Q16: How do you handle LLM service outages or rate limits?
A16: "`OpenAICompatibleLLMClient` includes exponential backoff retry logic and configurable
      timeouts (`LLM_TIMEOUT=30`, `LLM_MAX_RETRIES=2`). If the live LLM completely fails,
      the system gracefully raises a structured exception, logs the event, and prevents
      corrupted or half-written reports from being assembled."

Q17: What is ALCOA+ in regulatory data integrity, and how does PharmaVigil align with it?
A17: "ALCOA+ is the FDA standard for data integrity:
      - Attributable: Review records capture exact reviewer name and timestamp.
      - Legible: Formatted markdown and structured JSON outputs.
      - Contemporaneous: Timestamps generated at UTC execution time.
      - Original: Source ICSR records preserved; deterministic operations are reproducible.
      - Accurate: Math is computed deterministically; numbers are regex-verified."

Q18: How does single-section regeneration work without corrupting the rest of the report?
A18: "When a reviewer flags a single section (e.g. Narrative Summary), `regenerate_section()`
      takes the existing report object, pulls the specific context packet for that section,
      invokes the generator, validates grounding, and replaces ONLY that section in the
      sections list. It preserves all other 8 sections intact, but critically resets the
      overall report status to `DRAFT_PENDING_REVIEW`."

Q19: How did you ensure CORS security when connecting GitHub Pages to Render?
A19: "In `app.py`, FastAPI's `CORSMiddleware` is configured to allow cross-origin requests.
      In production enterprise environments, `allow_origins` can be locked down to the
      exact GitHub Pages domain (`https://ameydongre10.github.io`)."

Q20: Why did you use Pydantic v2 instead of standard Python dictionaries?
A20: "Pydantic v2 enforces runtime type safety, schema validation, and serialization.
      In regulatory compliance, data models like `AnalysisResult`, `EvidenceItem`,
      and `PADERReport` must guarantee that field types (booleans, integers, UTC dates)
      cannot be corrupted or passed as arbitrary unstructured types."

Q21: How do you prevent prompt injection from malicious ICSR free-text fields?
A21: "By design, raw free-text fields are NEVER directly concatenated into LLM prompts.
      Instead, free-text fields are sanitized, tokenized, and aggregated into structured
      counts and categories in Phase 2 before being presented as key-value pairs in
      Phase 3 evidence packets."

Q22: What is the significance of the `automated_evaluation_status` field?
A22: "It separates machine verification from human authorization. Even if the automated
      score is 100% (`automated_evaluation_status = PASS`), the document remains a
      draft. In regulated medical software (SaMD), machine verification is an advisory
      tool; the human clinician retains final legal responsibility."

Q23: How do you handle non-standard date formats or missing dates in ICSR files?
A23: "`pd.to_datetime(df['receivedate'], format='%Y%m%d', errors='coerce')` parses
      dates gracefully. Invalid or corrupted date values are coerced to `NaT` (Not a Time),
      and if the date range cannot be computed, the system reports `start_date: None`
      with an explicit data quality warning rather than crashing."

Q24: How does the containerized deployment work?
A24: "The `Dockerfile` is based on `python:3.11-slim`. It uses layer caching for
      `requirements.txt`, installs dependencies with `--no-cache-dir`, creates an
      unprivileged working directory, sets `PYTHONUNBUFFERED=1`, exposes port 8000,
      and includes a native `HEALTHCHECK` probing `/api/health` via curl."
